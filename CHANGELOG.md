# Changelog

## Prototype 37
The balance changes the Prototype 36 study suggested, checked on 1,700 simulated races run on the same tracks with the same frames and modules as before (1,000 with random module pairs, 500 comparing frames, 200 with the Hauler). Fair share below means a frame or module wins as often as an average racer (1.00).
- A frame out of phase, or hit in the last 2.5 s, leaves a jump's lip at least fast enough to clear the gap (with a level nose and a margin), so a hit just before a jump no longer drops you into it. A hit in the air still can. Falls: 0.20 to 0.05 a race with random modules, 0.56 to 0.09 in the frame races; on the worst jump of the study, 17 falls in 6 races became 1.
- Hauler: pick-up 0.95 to 1.0 and top speed 0.985 to 0.995. A point of thrust was the stat worth most, so pick-up is where it gained most. Top speed stays just under 1, which keeps the slower frames' harder hits and faster recharge. With equal builds it won 0.53 of a fair share and now wins 1.07; the fighting Hauler finishes 4.45th on average (was 4.75th). Its description no longer says it's slow to get going.
- Overdrive: 3 s (was 3.5), 6% faster (was 8%) and 60 ammo (was 50). It won 1.89 times a fair share and now wins 1.21. A shorter, slower Overdrive alone didn't move it: being untouchable is what wins, so it now fires less often.
- The five modules that won least got more slowing rather than more damage. A first try with more damage put a wreck more into every race and made Barrier and Decoy stronger (blocking hits is worth more when hits hurt more), so the damage stays as it was:
  - Cannon: each bolt that hits keeps 94% of the speed (was 96%). 0.62 to 0.67 of a fair share.
  - Shockwave: reaches 40 m (was 36) and leaves 72% of the speed (was 78%). 0.62 to 0.91.
  - Quake: costs 35 (was 40) and leaves 85% of the speed (was 90%). 0.72 to 0.80.
  - EMP: costs 25 (was 30) and holds top speed to 78% (was 82%). 0.72 to 0.78.
  - Smoke: flying in costs 8% of your speed (was 4%). 0.60 to 0.70.
- What else moved: Decoy rises to 1.76 times a fair share (from 1.47) and Barrier to 1.56 (from 1.47), taking what Overdrive gave up; they are now the modules that win most. Wrecks rise a little, 7.2 to 7.55 a race; no bug flags in the 1,700 races. Races with modules run about 0.7% longer (the winner 0.9 s on a 2-minute race), from the extra slowing and less Overdrive.
- Shared logs are sent as version 37. The board era stays 34: the winner's time moves by under 1%, less than the spread between good runs. Raising it to 37 would start fresh boards if you'd rather.

## Prototype 36
- The craft are called frames now, everywhere you read it: 8 frames · 3 laps, the Frame lab (it was the Ship lab), Your frame in Training, the career frame, and the module and graphics descriptions. Picking a Blade, Tether and so on in the Frame lab is still under Frame.
- A hit that used to spin you out now knocks your frame out of phase instead (rockets, missiles, the Hunter, Bouncer, mines, the Quake). Nothing about what it does has changed: you're slowed the same and other frames still pass through you until it settles. What you see: the frame flickers out of sight, tears sideways and stretches, shakes a little, and sheds small purple cubes of every size, some hanging by it as wisps and the rest streaming back as a trail; its shield shimmers purple. No more spinning, barrel rolls or hops. On your own frame the screen also tears in purple bands for a moment (with the screen effects on), so you see it in the cockpit view too. The camera keeps to your true line, so only the frame shakes, not the view. The cubes are on in every look (half as many with Low detail) and are one draw call.
- A frame already knocked out of phase takes the damage of another hit but isn't slowed again. Found by the 10,000 races and then in your own logs: a third of these hits land on a frame already out of phase, and each one cut its speed to 45% again, so two rockets of one volley left you at 20% of your speed and three at 9%. In your 42 races on Prototypes 34 and 35, 31% of the hits that stunned you came within 0.6 s of the one before; a double rocket or missile hit took you from about 150 m/s to around 30, sometimes to 2 to 9 m/s, and in the simulated races every frame that fell into a jump's gap had been hit in the second before the lip.
- Autopilot flies the frame, and your modules and boost stay yours: you can phase, fire or boost while it flies. It used to take over every input, so nothing fired.
- With a pad, the Frame lab tags your two fitted modules with the buttons that fire them (A and B) instead of 1 and 2. Every button name follows the pad you are using, read from the name the browser gives it: Xbox (A, B, X, Y, LB, RB, LT, RT), PlayStation (✕ ○ □ △ drawn as symbols, L1, R1, L2, R2, Options), Nintendo (by position: the bottom button is B; L, R, ZL, ZR, +) and the Steam Deck (A, B, X, Y in plain grey, L1, R1, L2, R2). Race logs now say which kind of pad was used and its name, so the next logs show what the Deck reports.
- The frame rate cap starts at 20 fps (it was 15); a cap of 15 moves to 20. A race step is held to 50 ms, so at 15 the whole race ran at three quarters speed and the times still counted. 20 alone wasn't quite enough: on a 90 Hz screen a 20 cap draws alternate frames 44 and 56 ms apart and the race ran at 94% (98% at 144 and 165 Hz). With a cap on, a frame can now run to 1.5 times the cap's interval, and 20 runs at full speed on 60, 90, 120, 144 and 165 Hz screens. With no cap nothing changes: a device that can't manage 20 fps still slows down below it.
- An Overdrive ram's shove stops at the wall. It could put the frame it hit up to 3 m into the wall for a frame (found by the simulated races).
- Shared logs are sent as version 36. The board era stays 34: the stun fix moves finishing times by about 0.3 s (see the study below) and nothing else changes the tracks or the handling.

  - The slowest track to build, SXLEB0AA7, takes 1.4 s (the median is 0.17 s, and it's 1 track in 3,300). Slow, but not a hang.

## Prototype 35
- Fixed the camera leaving your ship under a bridge (SL9D6FDD-R). Where a split's long route crosses over the short one, a ship on the short route was drawn up on the bridge for a moment (the bridge's line was nearer), 17 m above where it really was, and the camera went with it. Ships are now drawn on the route they are on; the light pools under them too. Ghosts and other players' ships in a room keep to the route they took.
- Both music styles at once. Drum & bass and Trance are now switches (both can be on; Off is on the Music page; the last style on stays on), on the menu and the Music page alike. With both on, the Music page offers:
  - Blend (the default): each tune is a mix of the two. Drum & bass or breakcore drums and basslines under a trance melody side: trance hooks and library lines, the lead carried through the breakdowns, harp runs, bells, claps, plucks answering, the trance instruments and supersaw arps. A blend is 174 bpm (trance & bass) or 138 bpm (breakbeat under trance), and the tune memory nudges them to take turns. Blend codes start with M (174) or N (138).
  - Random: each new tune is drum & bass or trance at random.
  - In turn: drum & bass and trance take turns, tune by tune.
  - With one style on, every track and tune code plays exactly the tune it did before.
- Frame rate cap, in Graphics: 15 to 360 fps, or no cap, where the game runs at the screen's own rate however high (the default, except Ultra low). A device that can't hold its screen's rate runs steadily at the cap instead of jumping between rates, and saves battery.
- Ultra low now draws at 20% (it was 25%) and caps at 30 fps. Anyone on Ultra low, or on their own look that is as light (everything off, Low detail, 25% or less), moves to it once (their own resolution is kept if it's already lower).
- Style is now called Graphics (the Settings section, its button and the page's title), and the looks are just Ultra low, Lite, Clean, Arcade and Everything (no "slowest tablets" or "for older phones").
- The game reads the screen size only when it changes, not every frame (the read made the browser lay the page out again each frame).
- From the logs (on Prototype 34: Steam Deck, 30 races and 8 drills; Doogee, 3 races):
  - The Deck held 60 fps in every race.
  - The Doogee, at 20% resolution with every part off, ran at 50 to 60 fps but dipped to 17 to 35 fps for 5 to 10 seconds at a time, at no particular place on the track but with the pack around you. At 20% the pixels aren't what slows it (draw calls and the processor are), so lower resolution won't help; the 30 fps cap holds it steady.
  - Road width is the difficulty, by design (Wide is easy: slower AI, more steering assist, softer walls; Narrow is hard). On the Deck you won most Wide races by a distance (often nobody else had finished when the results came up) and finished 5th to 8th on Narrow tracks, 8 to 36% behind the winner, with 100 to 175 wall touches a race. Left as it is for now.
- Shared logs are sent as version 35. The board era stays 34: nothing here changes the tracks or how the ships handle.

## Prototype 34
- Race page in a new order: Race (picked first), Multiplayer, Career, Time trial, Training, Ship lab.
- Training (Race, then Training): short tracks that repeat one thing, lap after lap. Seven drills: Hills (rolling hills down both straights, sized so a level nose takes off over the sharper crests and a dipped nose holds on), Chicanes (flat S-bends both ways), Hairpins (two tight hairpins, tighter than any race track's corners, so they need the brake), Jumps, Spirals, Loops & rolls, and Mixed (hills one side, S-bends the other). Wide, Standard or Narrow; 3, 5 or 10 laps; alone on the road with a ghost of your best run. Fly the Training cube, a plain stock ship that is the average of all eight frames with every stat at 3 and no special traits, or your own ship (Ship lab edits your own ship). Best laps and ghosts are kept per drill, width and ship; nothing goes to the online board, your profile or the career. Results offer Next drill. Training logs say mode training and the track code names the drill (for example T-HILLS-S). Crests on drill tracks can lift you off like on a complex track (simple race tracks never do).
- Weapons are now Modules everywhere in the menus (Ship lab, career, controls, button labels), since the scoops aren't weapons.
- Gamepad and keys: the focus remembers where it was. In the Ship lab, maxing or minimising a stat, picking a frame or picking a module keeps the focus on that control (it used to jump to Blade). The - and + at a stat's ends stay focusable, dimmed. Anywhere a menu is rebuilt under the focus, the focus goes to the same control, or the nearest one.
- Ship lab from the results: a Ship lab button next to Race again (not in a room, and not when the Training cube is in use). Done brings you back to the results, ready for Race again.
- Fences no longer stand in the road where routes split and rejoin (WQ11D96A-N, the cut in Eric's photo). Where two routes run side by side (a split's nose and tail, a pit lane or a cut leaving and rejoining) there is no wall between them, only painted edges, and you can drift across; the cut's exit no longer sweeps a wall across half the road; the 2 m hole in the road surface where a split rejoined is closed. The AI still takes cuts and pit lanes (it now brakes for a cut's sharp entry and holds its lane while the routes touch). Main roads are unchanged, career tracks included; pit and cut lanes are shaped a little differently.
- Off the track in the air you can fly back: about 2.6 times the usual air steering, and holding the brake turns you up to 2.5 times harder again (and slows you a little). A little more room before the respawn. In a test of fly-offs at a shallow angle, 100% got back to the road (it was 15%).
- Fixed a track that froze the game while building: when a layout's faults were in the base loop rather than its features, the retry loop never ended (WXQ6FFE5A, found by the balance study; about 1 track in 1,000).
- Fixed a freeze on a first time trial on a track: alone on the road, the music divided by zero and the frame loop stopped. The lap counter shows the race's laps (it always said 3).
- Music variety:
  - A memory of the last 16 tunes keeps things from coming round too often: a new tune is the least familiar of 12 candidates, so a theme, chord progression, library melody, blueprint, groove, sound, key, drum kit, instrument or percussion heard recently counts against it, more so the more recently. In 200 new tunes in a row, a theme from the last 5 came back 1% of the time (14 to 21% before); a chord progression from the last 3, 10 to 14% (30 to 32%); a library melody from the last 10, 1 to 3% (7 to 9%); a drum kit from the last 3, 15 to 17% (34 to 38%). In a room the track alone still decides, so everyone hears the same tune.
  - The library melodies were the biggest source of repeats: 67 of its 200 lines are the same 1-3-5-4-3-2-1 shape. A library line is now picked by melody first (every different melody equally likely), and the memory compares melodies, not entries.
  - 136 more CC0 recordings (about 770 KB; the page is about 2.3 MB): from the Versilian Community Sample Library, marimba, vibraphone, glockenspiel, kalimba, harpsichord, pipe organ, tenor sax, dan tranh, strumstick, balafon, alto recorder, a tubular bell, and hand percussion (bongos, congas, cajon, darbuka, tambourine, cowbell, agogo, woodblock, triangle, guiro, cabasa, frame drum, hi-hat, snare); from the Sonic Pi pack, kicks, snares, hats, electronic percussion, bass hits, tabla, pads, glitches and two breakbeats. Pitched ones are measured and tuned to the semitone.
  - What they do: seven new drum kits (808, club, electro, gas, tight, acoustic, tape; 1 tune in 8 keeps the original); eleven new instruments that take over where the piano or electric piano would play (by style: vibraphone, marimba, kalimba, tenor sax, pipe organ, harpsichord and an 808 cowbell in drum & bass; glockenspiel, strumstick and others in trance; dan tranh, balafon and recorder in psytrance, where half the tunes now get one); a percussion layer in half the tunes (congas, cajon, darbuka, tabla, agogo bells, electronic percussion, or a breakbeat stretched to the bar: the amen break or another); a bass hit in the key under each drop; a sub drop under the final lap's drop; a tubular bell in some trance drops; tuned pads and instrument sparkles in the menu music.
  - From Eric's racing-music guide, the parts that fit: seven new library lines that aren't the common shape (funk talk bass, house horn bass, jazzy minor-seventh keys, wide reese drop line, bitcrushed saw bass, eurobeat brass lead, staccato repeat lead), the 808 cowbell lead (drift phonk), the sub drop on the final lap (the nitro / final lap climax), and the new instruments high-passed at 160 Hz to keep the sub-bass lane clear.
  - Old tune codes play exactly as before: the new choices live in new bits of the code (0 is the old sound). Tune codes can be one digit longer. Lite audio doesn't load or play the new recordings.
- Resolution is a slider (Style), 10% to 100% in steps of 5. Ultra low now draws at 25% (it was a third); anyone on the old Ultra low moves to it once. Low detail also leaves out falls and geysers, whose particles move every frame.
- Balance, from about 3,300 simulated races (the balance report has the details; `tools/sim/` can run it again):
  - Speed did reign supreme: a point of speed saved 1.3% of race time against the average stat, and speed 5 builds won 19% of races. Top speed per point is now 3% (it was 4.5%) and turning per handling point 18% (it was 12%); a point of speed is now worth 0.35%, just behind thrust, and the gap between the best and worst stat halved.
  - Ammo regenerates by the ammo stat: none at 1, 0.1 a second at 2, a trickle (0.3) at 3, 1.0 at 4 and 2.2 at 5, times the frame's ammo factor. No extra strips, no other stat. The Ship lab shows the rate. Ammo 5 builds now win their fair share (6% before, 12% now) and the fighting Hauler finishes 0.6% behind the field (2.8% before).
  - Overdrive won 38% of races with it fitted (80% with Barrier too): now 3.5 s (was 4.5), ×1.08 top speed (was ×1.12), a ram does 16 damage and leaves 82% of the victim's speed (was 22 and 70%), and it costs 50 (was 40). Barrier lasts 3.5 s (was 6) and costs 35 (was 30). Shockwave reaches 36 m (was 30), does 26 damage (was 19) and leaves 78% speed (was 85%) for 30 ammo (was 35); EMP lasts 4.5 s (was 3.5) for 30 (was 35); Cannon costs 5 (was 6), Quake and Hunter 40 (were 45), Smoke 15 (was 20). The top modules now win about 21%.
  - Frames: the Tether has a little less shield (factor 0.78, was 0.85) and the Orb loses a quarter of its speed and shield to a wall hit (was a third) with a little more pick-up (1.02), both from Eric's hunches, which the races bore out; the Skiff has less pick-up (1.04, was 1.1) and shield (0.76, was 0.8); the Hauler (0.95, was 0.91) and Roller (1.0, was 0.97) more pick-up. With random builds every frame now wins between 72% and 130% of its fair share (it was 50% to 148%). The Training cube follows, being the average of the frames.
- Board era 34: cut and pit lanes build differently, and handling and the stats changed, so boards start fresh (old times are kept).
- Shared logs are sent as version 34.

## Prototype 33
- No more roads through roads, found by playtesting about 3,000 random tracks with a script that checks every road surface (both routes of every split, pit lane and cut too) against every other, the scenery, the signs and the gantries, then rendering each kind of fault found.
- Splits, pit lanes and cuts now end before the green repair strips at the end of the lap. About 1 track in 20 had a lane rejoining across the strips, its walls standing in them; from the cockpit it looks like a yellow road crossing the green-striped road (the overlap in the Oct 31 screenshots). About 3 track codes in 100 now build a different road (a lane moved or left out); career tracks are unchanged.
- Split and pit signs stand on plain, nearly level road. About 1 track in 5 had a sign upside down in a loop, on its side in a corkscrew, standing in another split's routes or on the start grid. A sign now moves nearer or further to find a good spot and shows the real distance (for example ROAD SPLITS · 450 M), or is left out; the 80 m sign is always there.
- Gantries: the search for a clear spot gave up after 800 m, so on a long helix or spire the gantry stood in it, once nearly on its side; now each searches to the next fifth of the lap, stays off steep banks and 30 m from the signs.
- Pylons: a crossover's legs could stand in its own short route (the check only knew the long routes). Now no pylon stands in any route.
- The tower, tree or column in the middle of a spiral could swallow another part of the lap passing through the middle (about 1 spiral in 35, mostly city towers). It now keeps 6 m clear, or is left out.
- Board era 33: some track codes build a different road, so boards start fresh (old times are kept).
- Shared logs are sent as version 33.

## Prototype 32
- Career (Race, then Career): four tiers of four races against the AI. Rookie is run at 70% speed on wide tracks with no climbs, drops or jumps; Club at 80% adds climbs, dives and a spire; Pro at 90% brings the first complex tracks; Elite is full speed on narrow complex tracks. A top-three finish clears a race, opens the next one and unlocks a weapon (you start with Missile, Cannon and Turbo; the AI only fires what you have unlocked). Every finish pays credits (600 for a Rookie win, up to 1,200 in Elite), and credits buy upgrade points for the career ship, which starts with 10 of the usual 15 (400 to 1,900 credits a point). The career ship is its own build in the Ship lab; your own ship and track come back when you leave. Jumps keep their shape at the slower speeds (gravity on a jump scales with the speed squared). Career times stay off the online board, ghosts and the free-race best laps; career race logs say mode career and the speed class.
- Menus: the title now holds only Race, Options and Leaderboard, plus a small music player (Drum & bass, Trance, Race music, Next and the song details). Race opens Career, Race, Time trial, Multiplayer and Ship lab. Race and Time trial open the track page: widths, layouts, lengths, scenery, Night and Day, New track, the track code and the map, with Start. Options holds Music, Settings, Controls and the Nose guide, from the title, the pause menu or a lobby (the lobby's Controls and Settings buttons became Options). The pause menu is Resume, Restart, Options and Menu; Keep this tune is in Music. On the results, New song, Keep this tune and Save race log are small buttons under Race again, New track and Menu.
- Layouts checked by screenshot on phones both ways round (360 to 915 wide), tablets both ways, the Steam Deck and desktops up to 2560 wide, and fixed where needed. On phones: the Music page's Keep button was off-screen and couldn't be reached (fixed); on landscape phones Career, Results, the Race page and Pause fit without scrolling; the track map spans the width in portrait; the Ship lab card sits under the ship in portrait; finger-sized chips in portrait. On big screens the menus and HUD grow with the screen; on long pages (Controls, Settings, Style) Done stays in view. The HUD numbers have a dark halo so they read over the white start gantry, and the HUD hides behind the pause and results cards. The cyan focus ring shows only when a pad or the keys are in use, and the pause menu opens on Resume.
- Fewer words in a race, more cues. Weapons, Boost, shield and ammo have icons (also in the Ship lab). Instead of notes: boost refused for want of shield shakes the shield gauge and the Boost button with a "no" sound; firing without the ammo shakes the ammo gauge and the weapon with the same sound; the ammo gauge glows purple while you can't afford a shot; a hit flashes the shield gauge; a wreck flashes both gauges red as the ammo halves; EMP makes the weapon icons and Boost flicker blue; the lap counter swells on each new lap (amber on the final lap) and the lap time shows under the clock for a few seconds. Gone: Airborne, Slipstream, EMP offline, Hit, Wrecked, Back in the race, Weapons armed, Out of ammo, Shield low and the respawn notes.
- Sound effects are real recordings now, like the music: 34 public-domain (CC0) recordings from Kenney's Interface, Digital Audio, Sci-Fi and Impact packs, rubberduck's CC0 packs on OpenGameArt and the Sonic Pi pack, for every weapon (Rockets and Hunter share the Missile recording at other speeds, Turbo the boost), boost, pads, jumps, landings, hits, wall hits, explosions and wrecks, the countdown, laps and the final lap, shield and ammo full, shield low, the "no" sound, and moving around the menus with a pad or keys. Each is set as loud as the synth sound it replaced, and the old synth sounds stay as the fallback. About 124 KB (the page is about 1.50 MB).
- Slipstream shows as the little lines round your ship (pale blue; green is still nose-down streamlining). Slipstream already worked behind any ship, the player included, for AI and for other pilots in a room.
- Light pools no longer clip through the road. They were one flat card turned with the ship, so steering roll and nose pitch tipped half of it into the road (about 30% of the glow hidden on every corner), and on dips, loops, twists and corkscrews it sank under the curve (all of it at the top of a loop). Now each pool is a small grid laid on the road under it, following crests, loops, twists and split routes, kept between the edges, fading as the ship rises off the road and hidden over jump gaps. Measured over an AI race on a complex track: clipping in 0 of 136 samples (it was 29% of 416). Still one draw call a ship.
- Ultra low now draws at a third of the resolution (it was half), the lowest setting; anyone on the old Ultra low moves to it once.
- Shared logs are sent as version 32.

## Prototype 31
- No more road through road. Every track is checked as it is built for parts of the lap passing through or nearly touching each other (spirals and spires included, at the road's edges, allowing for bank), kinks, and slopes over 42 degrees. If six layouts all fail, the features at fault are left out until the road is clean (before, the least bad layout was kept). Cause of the forest clash (SX813E2C): a spiral built on a steep descent came back over its own entry only 3.4 m up.
- No more right angles. The base loop eases any bend tighter than the width allows (Wide 29, Standard 38, Narrow 44 degrees in any 30 m) and any sharp change of slope. Rises, drops and lifts move the road straight up (a tilted "up" made spiral exits step backwards, up to 175 degrees in 12 m). Spirals and spires start and end at the road's own slope. A spire rejoins the road by a smooth curve chosen for the gentlest bend (it used to shift the old line up to 250 m sideways, which folded the road and threw it 90 m up and down). Climbs are at most about 31 degrees, and less on a slope. Loops are coaster loops now: the bend builds from gentle at the foot to tightest at the top. Over 500 random tracks: no clashes, at most 26 degrees in any 12 m and 51 in any 30 m (was up to 179), steepest 40 degrees (was 90).
- Board era 31: the track changes make older times unfair, so boards start fresh (old times are kept).
- Wi-Fi, no internet (Multiplayer, Connection): race on one Wi-Fi from any copy of the game, itch.io included, with no server. The host shows a QR code; each pilot scans it (Join a Wi-Fi room) and shows a reply for the host to scan; a device without a camera types the code. The host relays the race, so the host must stay in the room. The Wi-Fi host pack still works as before.
- Ultra low look (Style options): Lite at half resolution with Low detail, for the slowest tablets. New Resolution (Full, three quarters, half, a third) and Low detail (no ship shadows, thinner scenery) controls. Every look races the same, so times count whichever you pick.
- Lighter for every look: each racing ship's parts are merged (about 40 draw calls a ship down to 8), and the pylons are one instanced mesh (a long Ocean track went from 341 draw calls a frame to 92).
